The phone bridge, drawn from its code

An old Android phone is a robot's face, eyes and ears. It runs a web page; the atl_phone bridge, a ROS 2 node in Docker, serves that page and maps its WebSocket to topics under /<robot>/phone/ (contract atl-phone/0.6). The brain stays on the PC, or on the robot's Raspberry Pi. These thirteen diagrams show how a phone gets connected and tested, what the bridge remembers, where it can go wrong, the Desk Guardian (a ROS 2 node that knows only topics), what flows between the phone and ROS 2, how the real phone and its Webots twin plug into the bridge, where each part runs in the lab and on the robot with its Pi, and which recognition runs on the phone and which on the Pi. The bridge serves this pack at /docs/; the setup page's How it works view links it.

Every box cites the lines it comes from, at commit c098351; the build checked each citation against that commit.

The whole setup across this PC, the bridge and the phone, with the three places it stops: port taken, a refused WebSocket, a page that is not secure.

02What the bridge keeps in memory data model

The current Link, the phone's hello, the senses seen and the check reports, and since 0.5–0.6 what a phone that comes gets again, the show state and the screen report: nothing on disk.

03One setup test, there and back sequence

An output test through the contract's own ROS topic, the camera test straight to the phone, and the poll that ticks the page.

04The phone's link to the bridge lifecycle

Start screen, handshake, ready; dropped links retry at 1 s up to 5 s; a phone that was taken over stays quiet.

05A second phone takes over sequence · race

One frame already in flight from the old phone used to land as the new phone's; the swap and the delivery now share one lock.

06The Desk Guardian's states lifecycle

Guarding, alert, friend, intruder; lifted and the desk's edge come first. Friends are face numbers, forgotten when the phone forgets.

07The guardian sees a face, then its owner sequence

Phone, bridge, guardian and Thymio, topic by topic: where the face is goes in, a turn of the wheels comes out; pictures and signatures never leave the phone.

08What flows between the phone and ROS 2 data flow

Senses, vision and picks are checked and clamped, rate-limited and published; programs answer on the in-topics, whose makers check again. What never reaches ROS: frames, the screen report, the location.

09The real phone and its Webots twin, wired architecture

USB through the host's adb server, the two gates /ws and /ws/mirror, the phone card through adb's own protocol, and the twin as the phone or as its mirror, beside the Thymio twin.

10A tap on the phone, drawn by the twin sequence

The twin joins and gets the current screen; a tap, a tile and a shown letter travel as keys, a ROS topic and, with consent, loopback-only frames.

11The lab, now: everything on the PC architecture

The phone on the PC's USB, the bridge and its programs in one container, the setup page and the docs from the bridge, Webots with the twin as a mirror beside it.

12Autonomous: the Pi on the robot architecture

Designed, not yet run on the Pi: the same image on the Pi, the phone and the Thymio on its USB, and a PC or tablet reaching the loopback-only setup page through an SSH tunnel.

13Recognition: the phone sees, the Pi decides architecture

MediaPipe, face-api, js-aruco2, the browser's QR reader and Tesseract.js on the phone; on the Pi, checked numbers only, which the Desk Guardian and Omi Assist act on.

After a takeover, a frame the old phone had sent before the close was still parsed and credited to the new phone (on_text did not know which link a message came from), so a late hello could overwrite the new phone's model. Now deliver() passes a frame on only while its link is current, under the same lock as the takeover. A test reproduces the race. wsserver.py 206–225, 361–364 · 05 fixed in 6b29b5a
Output tests (face, gaze, colour, say, torch, vibrate) answered 204 even with no phone, because out() ignores the result of server.send. It misled the first real-phone test (a Galaxy A50): the phone was on the check page while its outputs were tried. act() now refuses them with "no phone", and step 4 shows whether a phone is connected. bridge_node.py 414–416 · 03 fixed in d2b04ac
Between a disconnect and the next phone, /api/state still shows the old senses: closed() clears the phone, not seen. The setup page relies on connected, and keeps what passed once, so its marks stay right. bridge_node.py 210–222, setup.js 777–793 · 04 by design
A check report was matched to the connected phone by model text only. Two phones of a kind looked the same, and the first real phone did not match itself: its hello named it SM-A505FN (client hints), its report "Android 10 phone" (the user agent says K), so the page fell back to the latest report of any phone. Now the face and check pages keep a random id in the browser and both messages carry it. face.js 428–437, bridge_node.py 449–457, setup.js 401–407 · 02 fixed in 9861131
The state dicts are shared between threads without a lock. A stress test (200 000 json.dumps of a dict while another thread adds keys, Python 3.12.3 in the image) raised no error; the bridge replaces values, it does not mutate them in place. bridge_node.py 247–353 · 02 checked · no issue
A phone on plain http over Wi-Fi gets no camera, motion, compass or battery; it now says so in its hello and the setup page marks them blocked, with the fix (USB, or --cert). face.js 438–443, setup.js 788–793 · 01 handled in e8570a6
Wi-Fi with camera and motion needs HTTPS. ./start.sh --make-cert makes this PC's own certificate authority once; the bridge offers /ca.crt and each phone installs it once. The authority's key is not mounted into the container. A phone that has the authority installed trusts anything this PC's key signs, so the setup page says how to remove it when a phone leaves the class. scripts/make_cert.sh, start.sh 84–93, wsserver.py 259–270 · 01 added in d2b04ac
Telling faces apart ("familiar") is a signature kept in the phone's page only. A program can ask for it but not allow it: the facilitator does (familiar:=true or the setup page), and until then the bridge sets every face's number to null, whatever the phone sends. The contract check sends a number and a 128-number descriptor and sees neither reach ROS. bridge_node.py 130–139, 287–294, protocol.py 164–178 · 02 by design
The phone reuses face numbers once it forgets its signatures (familiar off, page reloaded). The guardian kept number 1 as its friend, so a second run of its story greeted a new face as the owner. It now forgets its friends when the bridge's status shows the phone forgot. guardian_node.py 109–115, guardian.py 118–121 · 06 fixed in a259a7a
The guardian, in the bridge's container, heard nothing from atl-bot-thymio's: Fast DDS uses shared memory between processes on one host, and containers do not share /dev/shm. start.sh now makes it use UDP. start.sh 94–96 · 07 fixed in a259a7a
Both ground sensors over nothing count as lifted, so a Thymio that reaches the desk's edge head-on stops and asks to be put down instead of backing off (atl-bot-thymio's behaviours read it the same way). Safe, but not the cliff reaction. The guardian itself never drives forward, so it reaches an edge only by turning. guardian.py 88–102 · 06 open · minor
While the robot is lifted or backing off from the edge, a long touch is not kept for later: petting during those 2.2 s is lost. guardian.py 153–177 · 06 open · minor
Following faces and hands at about 3 searches a second kept the ANE-LX1 at a load average of about 40. Omi now follows faces only, at 4 searches a second with someone in view and 1 with nobody. The browser check tests the pace; the phone's load after the change is not measured yet. see.js 21, 323–326, omi_assist.py 29 · 08 fixed in 4220c01
A phone keeps running the page's old scripts after the bridge restarts: it reconnects to /ws with what it loaded, and the bridge has no way to make it reload. Fix: the bridge's first message carries a hash of its web files, and the page reloads when it differs. Seen on 2026-10-26. Now each hello is answered with the pages' fingerprint; a page from an older bridge reloads, started without a tap, and the setup page does the same with /api/state (contract check and browser check). bridge_node.py 237, face.js 481–485 · 11 fixed in c098351
The twin in --mirror mode stops when the bridge closes the mirror socket, so a bridge restart leaves Webots with a frozen phone until the twin is started again. Fix: retry like the page does (1 s to 5 s). Seen on 2026-10-26. Now it shows the phone away and joins again every 2 s; tested across a restart of the live bridge ("the bridge closed the mirror; trying again", then "mirror back"). atl_phone_twin.py 802–816 · 11 fixed in c098351
The ontology says the setup page is opened over Wi-Fi "with the pairing key", but /api answers loopback only, whatever the key: from a PC on the robot's Wi-Fi the setup page loads and then sees no bridge. Today: an SSH tunnel to the Pi makes the PC loopback. Fix: correct the ontology's wording, or add a key-gated admin route if a tunnel is too much for the people using it. Read, not reproduced (no Pi yet). omi_robot.ttl 122, wsserver.py 308–317 · 12 open · minor
A frame she shows (Zeigen) goes to loopback mirrors and the setup page only. With the bridge on the robot's Pi, a PC over Wi-Fi gets no pictures, neither on its setup page nor in its Webots twin, unless it comes through an SSH tunnel, which makes it loopback. wsserver.py 190–196, 308–317 · 10 by design
The screen report and the sensor numbers carry ids, text keys and clamped numbers only: screen_report() drops an unknown key, a toast's argument must be a known object, and there is no field for the location. Two tests send free text and a location and see neither pass (test_protocol.py 250–275); the browser check sees only the four sensor keys in the page's report. protocol.py 241–280 · 10 by design
The phone card needs adb, but the container has none: a second adb of another version would kill the host's server and the phone's USB link. phone_health speaks adb's protocol to the host's server instead, and never offers or closes the bridge's own tab (tested against a fake adb and a fake phone Chrome). phone_health.py 5–7, 145–159 · 09 by design
